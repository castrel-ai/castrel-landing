A complex incident rarely leaves the team with one question. When a database primary stops accepting writes, responders need to identify the immediate cause while checking replica health, business impact, and gaps in monitoring. Those lines of work have often ended up in separate chats, tickets, or temporary documents. Each responder rebuilds the background, while the incident owner repeatedly asks every workstream for an update.

The latest version of Castrel AI lets one incident contain multiple parallel investigation tasks. Team members can start from the same description, impact scope, and related alerts while pursuing different questions. Progress, findings, and reports from each task return to the same incident context. The investigation can move in parallel without turning one incident into several disconnected records.

## One database incident can raise several questions at once

Consider a production PostgreSQL incident. The available investigation found that the `PG_DATA` volume for the former CloudNativePG primary, `postgres-cluster-1`, had a capacity of only 50 GiB. After the volume ran out of space, the instance manager detected the low-disk condition and refused to start the instance. The Pod entered `CrashLoopBackOff`, interrupting database writes for approximately 1.5 hours.

After the volume was expanded, `postgres-cluster-2` was promoted to primary and the former primary returned as a replica. The investigation also identified a separate problem: the production namespace lacked the relevant Pod metrics, volume metrics, and alert rules, so the team received no early warning before the disk was exhausted.

With a single investigation task, primary recovery, replica catch-up, alert coverage, and business-impact validation compete for one execution path. The team either waits for each step or divides the work verbally outside the task. Verbal division may create concurrency, but it also separates evidence from decisions and makes verified findings difficult to distinguish from open questions.

In Castrel AI, the team can create several investigation tracks under the same incident:

| Investigation track | Question to answer | Result returned to the incident |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary and storage | Why did the disk fill, did expansion complete, and why could the instance not start? | PVC capacity changes, Kubernetes events, and the immediate cause |
| Replication and failover | Is the new primary stable, and has the former primary caught up as a replica? | Role changes, replication state, and open validation items |
| Business impact | Which services were affected by the write interruption, and are requests healthy after recovery? | Impact scope, recovery status, and follow-up observations |
| Monitoring coverage | Why was there no early alert, and which collectors or rules are missing? | Monitoring gaps, corrective actions, and acceptance criteria |

These tasks can begin at the same time. Database, Kubernetes, and observability owners do not need to wait for a master task to reach their step, and they do not need to copy the incident description into every assignment.

## Shared context gives every investigation a useful starting point

The cost of parallel response usually comes from synchronizing context rather than assigning work. When one incident becomes several independent tickets, each ticket repeats the event time, affected object, known alerts, and current recovery state. A change to the background must then be copied everywhere.

Castrel AI keeps the incident as the shared parent context. Investigation tasks inherit the description, impact scope, and related alerts, so a new task can begin with information the team has already established. In the PostgreSQL scenario, every investigation can see the exhausted primary volume, write interruption, and role change. The replication owner does not need to identify the incident object again, while the monitoring owner can compare missing telemetry with the actual incident window.

Shared context does not force every task toward the same conclusion. Each investigation keeps its own objective, working process, and report. The common layer contains the background and evidence entry points; the separate tasks contain the questions that still need verification. This structure reduces repeated preparation and prevents one workstream from relying on an outdated incident description.

## Findings return to the incident instead of remaining inside tasks

Once work runs in parallel, the incident owner needs a combined view rather than more windows. Castrel AI brings task status, investigation findings, and reports into the incident detail, where the team can see which directions are confirmed, which remain under review, and what happens next.

This differs from assembling a summary from chat logs after the event. Results enter the incident context while the work is underway. The storage investigation may confirm that the 50 GiB volume was expanded to 500 GiB. The high-availability investigation can record that `postgres-cluster-2` has taken the primary role while replica WAL catch-up still requires validation. The monitoring investigation can preserve the missing production metrics and alert rules as follow-up work. The incident owner can then evaluate closure conditions without polling every task owner.

The incident detail also keeps an organized operational record. Team members can add comments with manual confirmations, risk judgments, or handoff notes. A responder joining later can quickly separate confirmed conclusions from open directions and assigned next actions.

## Handoffs start with the next verification, not a replay of the incident

Shift changes and temporary reinforcements expose fragmented information quickly. The incoming responder often spends the first part of the handoff finding alert links, reading chats, and asking which actions have already been performed. More workstreams mean more reconstruction.

A unified incident context changes that starting point. The incoming responder can review impact, related alerts, task states, reports, operational records, and comments, then continue from the remaining validation item. The previous owner does not have to retell the full sequence, and the incident lead can retain a complete view while tasks progress in parallel.

For this PostgreSQL incident, service recovery did not complete every line of work. Replica catch-up, production telemetry, and alert validation still required attention. Keeping those tracks under the same incident distinguishes “writes have recovered” from “the remaining operational risk is closed,” so the recovery action does not hide unfinished reliability work.

## One incident record preserves the team's investigation process

Multiple investigation tasks address the collaboration structure of a complex incident. Castrel AI lets specialists work on separate questions at the same time while preserving a shared factual baseline. Evidence and conclusions return to the incident detail, while the operational record and comments retain the decisions behind them.

The next responder receives an incident record that is ready to continue: the current impact, confirmed conclusions, risks that remain open, and the next facts to verify.
